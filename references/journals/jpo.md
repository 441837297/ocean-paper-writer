# JPO

Journal of Physical Oceanography

---

## 1. Journal Narrative Identity

JPO is a **dynamics-first, mechanism-first physical oceanography journal**.
It does not publish descriptive studies, multi-evidence surveys, or data reports unless they are organized around a dynamical question.
It publishes research where the central contribution is a physical mechanism, a dynamical process, or a theory-guided explanation of an oceanographic phenomenon.

In ocean science, JPO is the right home when you have:

- A result that can be explained by a physical mechanism — not just observed, but understood in terms of dynamics, balances, instabilities, forcing, or transport pathways
- A process-oriented diagnosis — momentum balances, vorticity budgets, energy conversions, water-mass transformations, wave dynamics, or scale interactions that reveal why the system behaves as it does
- An observational, modeling, or theoretical study where the physical explanation is the primary contribution, not the data description or the methodological advance

JPO is **not** the right home when:

- The central finding is descriptive and the physical mechanism is not diagnosed — the result can be stated without answering "why it happens"
- The evidence chain is complete but the dynamical insight is thin — the paper shows what happened with multiple lines of evidence but does not explain the controlling physics
- The contribution is primarily about data, methods, or regional characterization without a process-level question

The core narrative personality of JPO is: **mechanism-seeking, process-diagnosing, dynamics-grounded**.
A successful JPO paper takes the reader from a physical question, through diagnostic evidence, to a dynamical interpretation.
The contribution is measured by how much the reader's physical understanding advances, not by how many datasets were assembled or how many figures are shown.

## 2. Best-fit Ocean Science Stories

### Dynamical mechanism story

A study that identifies and diagnoses a physical mechanism controlling an ocean process — for example, which instability feeds the eddy field in a boundary current, how topographic steering modifies the cross-shelf circulation, or what balance sets the stratification in a mode-water formation region.
The contribution is the mechanism itself, supported by process diagnostics.

### Process diagnosis story

A study organized around a quantitative diagnosis — momentum or vorticity budget closure, energy conversion analysis, heat/salt/freshwater budget, mixed-layer temperature or salinity budget, or potential vorticity analysis.
The diagnosis is the primary analytical framework, and the results are presented through the lens of what the budget reveals about the controlling physics.

### Scaling or balance story

A study that uses scaling analysis, non-dimensional numbers, regime diagrams, or dominant-balance arguments to explain why a system behaves as it does.
These papers often combine observations or model output with theoretical scaling to show that the observed behavior is consistent with a specific dynamical regime.

### Instability, eddy, wave, or boundary-process story

A study focused on a specific physical process — baroclinic or barotropic instability, eddy-mean flow interaction, internal wave generation and breaking, frontogenesis, mixed-layer instabilities, topographic Rossby waves, coastal trapped waves, or tidal dynamics at boundaries.
The process is the organizing principle, and the evidence is structured around diagnosing it.

### Theory-guided observational story

An observational study designed to test, constrain, or validate a physical theory.
The observations are important, but the contribution lies in what they reveal about the theory — whether it holds, where it breaks down, or what modifications are needed.
These papers typically state the theoretical prediction first, then present observations that evaluate it.

### Model-observation mechanism story

A study where observations identify a phenomenon, and a numerical model or idealized experiment is used to diagnose the mechanism.
The model is not an end in itself — it is a tool for isolating and quantifying the physical process.
The paper succeeds when the model helps explain what the observations cannot reveal alone.

### Stories that belong elsewhere:

| Better fit for | Story characteristics |
|---------------|----------------------|
| GRL | One sharp finding with limited but sufficient evidence, shorter mechanism chain |
| JGR-Oceans | Multi-evidence completeness, regional description, validation-focused, mechanism plausible but not necessarily diagnosed |
| Nature Communications | Cross-disciplinary significance, narrative-driven, accessible to non-specialist scientists |
| JAOT or ESSD | Method or data product description, technical validation, no dynamical hypothesis required |
| Journal of Climate | Climate-scale variability, forced change attribution, large-scale teleconnections, statistical atmospheric-ocean coupling |

## 3. Preferred Reasoning and Evidence Logic

### Claim structure

JPO claims are **mechanism claims**.
A claim that "this process occurs" is a starting point; the paper must also claim "this process occurs *because* of a specific dynamical mechanism." The central claim should be a physical explanation, not a description.

Evidence in JPO is evaluated by how well it supports the mechanism:

- **Direct process diagnostics** (budget closure, energy conversion terms, vorticity balance) provide the strongest support for mechanism claims
- **Correlative evidence** (spatiotemporal patterns consistent with a mechanism) is weaker and should be accompanied by acknowledgment of alternative explanations
- **Theoretical or scaling consistency** strengthens a claim when the observed behavior matches a predicted dynamical regime
- **Model experiments** (sensitivity tests, idealized configurations, process-oriented simulations) can isolate mechanisms that observations alone cannot distinguish

### How much evidence is enough

JPO requires **enough diagnostic evidence to convince a specialist that the mechanism is correctly identified**.
Key principles:

- **One well-closed budget is stronger than ten suggestive patterns.** A single quantitative budget that demonstrates the dominant terms carries more weight than multiple figures showing consistent spatial patterns.
- **Diagnostics must be appropriate to the question.** A claim about eddy energy pathways requires energy conversion diagnostics (KmKe, PeKe, etc.), not just EKE maps.
A claim about frontal dynamics requires frontogenesis or deformation diagnostics, not just SST gradient maps.
- **Sensitivity tests strengthen mechanism claims.** When model or analysis choices (parameter values, filtering thresholds, coordinate systems) could affect the conclusion, sensitivity tests show that the mechanism is robust.
- **Alternative explanations should be considered, not ignored.** A mechanism claim is stronger when the paper explicitly acknowledges and rules out plausible alternatives.

### Mechanism language

Mechanism language must be matched to the diagnostic evidence:

| Evidence type | Language | Example |
|---------------|----------|---------|
| Direct process diagnostics (budget closure, energy conversion, PV analysis) | "demonstrates", "shows that", "reveals that" | "The vorticity budget demonstrates that bottom torque balances the planetary vorticity advection over the slope." |
| Strong pattern with physical consistency but no direct budget | "is consistent with", "suggests", "indicates" | "The spatial pattern of EKE suggests that baroclinic instability is the dominant energy source." |
| Scaling or regime-based reasoning | "is consistent with", "supports the interpretation that" | "The observed scaling of mixing efficiency with the Richardson number supports the interpretation that shear-driven mixing dominates." |
| Plausible mechanism not directly diagnosed | "may reflect", "could be related to", "might be explained by" | "The seasonal modulation may reflect changes in the background stratification." |

**Inappropriate even in JPO:** Claiming a specific mechanism from spatiotemporal correlation alone without addressing alternative dynamical explanations, or using causal language ("drives", "controls") when the diagnostic chain is incomplete.

### Ocean science-specific evidence logic for JPO

- **Momentum vs vorticity vs energy perspective:** The choice of diagnostic framework should be justified by the physical question.
A boundary current separation problem may require vorticity analysis; an eddy-driven flux problem may require energy diagnostics; a mixing problem may require TKE or buoyancy flux analysis.
- **Reference frame and averaging:** The choice of coordinate system (isopycnal vs fixed depth), averaging period, and filtering method affects the diagnosed balances.
These choices should be justified, not assumed.
- **Model fidelity for mechanism studies:** An idealized model that captures the essential dynamics may be more effective for mechanism diagnosis than a high-resolution realistic model whose complexity obscures the causal chain.
Model limitations should be explicitly connected to their impact on the mechanism claim.
- **Observational sampling and representativeness:** For observational mechanism studies, the representativeness of the sampling period (one cruise, one mooring year) must be addressed.
Is the diagnosed mechanism active only during the observed period, or is it a persistent feature?
- **Regional vs generalizable mechanisms:** A regional mechanism claim can be a significant contribution if the dynamics are transferable.
The paper should state what sets the regime — which parameters, forcing conditions, or geometric constraints control whether the mechanism applies elsewhere.

### Uncertainty handling

- Uncertainties should be quantified where possible (error bars on budget residuals, sensitivity to thresholds, ensemble spread).
- Budget residuals should be reported and discussed — a non-zero residual does not invalidate a budget, but ignoring it weakens the mechanism claim.
- Model-based mechanism claims should include sensitivity tests or ensemble diagnostics to show robustness.
- Limitations should focus on what the diagnostic framework cannot resolve, not on generic caveats.

## 4. Section-level Story Strategy

### Introduction

JPO Introduction should establish a **dynamical question**, not just a knowledge gap.
The gap should be framed in physical terms — e.g., "the dynamical balance controlling the separation latitude remains unclear" or "the mechanism by which topographic roughness dissipates internal tide energy is debated." This is stronger than "few studies have examined."

A strong JPO Introduction arc:
(1) establish the physical process and what is known about its dynamics;
(2) identify the unresolved dynamical question — which balance, mechanism, or regime is unclear, what competing hypotheses exist;
(3) state what this study diagnoses and how;
(4) preview the key mechanism finding.

Avoid:
generic climate opening, exhaustive literature review, or framing the gap as "few studies have examined" without explaining why the dynamics are unresolved.

### Methods

Methods in JPO should be organized around **how each choice supports the mechanism diagnosis**:
data or model description sufficient for a specialist to evaluate the diagnosis;
the diagnostic framework (budget equations, energy conversion terms, scaling analysis, or process diagnostics) with each method connected to the physical question;
processing and filtering choices (eddy-mean decomposition, coordinate systems, averaging windows) justified for their impact on diagnosed balances;
and uncertainty or sensitivity tests that establish robustness.

Methods can have subsections organized by diagnostic step.
A standalone "Study Area" section is not expected — regional context should be woven into the data description or introduced in context of why the region is dynamically instructive.

### Results

JPO Results should progress from **pattern to process to mechanism**:
(1) observed or simulated pattern that establishes what needs to be explained;
(2) diagnostic decomposition — which terms dominate the budget, which energy pathway sustains the field, which instability is active;
(3) mechanism synthesis — how the diagnostics explain the pattern.

Key principles:
- Lead each subsection with the diagnostic finding, not the figure reference
- Figures should serve the diagnostic narrative — if a figure does not advance the mechanism understanding, it belongs in supplementary material
- Quantitative values should include units and, where appropriate, the leading-order terms
- Secondary findings can appear if they support the mechanism interpretation, but should not dominate

### Discussion

JPO Discussion should **complete the mechanism argument**:
(1) restate the central dynamical finding in physical terms;
(2) address alternative dynamical explanations and show why the evidence favors the proposed mechanism;
(3) compare with theoretical predictions and previous diagnostic studies, explaining agreement or disagreement in terms of dynamics;
(4) bound the mechanism — under what forcing, stratification, topographic, or parameter conditions does it apply;
(5) implications for broader understanding of circulation, transport, mixing, or variability.

JPO Discussion can be technically detailed.
It should not restate Results.
It should not expand into implications that are not connected to the diagnosed mechanism.

### Conclusion

JPO Conclusion should close the mechanism story: restate the physical problem and diagnostic approach, summarize the mechanism finding with supporting evidence, state the dynamical significance.
Optionally include 1-2 sentences on where the mechanism may apply or what diagnostic question remains.
Do not introduce new diagnostic results, repeat Methods, or expand into speculative implications beyond what the mechanism supports.

### Abstract

JPO Abstract should communicate the **dynamical contribution**: physical problem → diagnostic approach → mechanism finding → dynamical implication.
Start with the physical question, state the diagnostic method briefly, present the key mechanism finding with quantification, and end with what this means for ocean dynamics.
A successful JPO Abstract reads like an answer to a dynamical question, not a data report.

## 5. Writing Voice and Rhetorical Style

### Sentence and paragraph rhythm

JPO writing is **technical but clear**.
Paragraphs are 4-8 sentences, each advancing one step of the diagnostic or interpretive narrative.
Use active voice: "The vorticity budget shows" not "It is shown by the vorticity budget." Avoid filler phrases ("It should be noted that").
Transitions should reflect the logical progression of the diagnostic argument, not just sequence.

### Claim strength and hedging

Claims should be **precise and diagnostic-bounded**.
Budget closure or direct process measures support "demonstrates" or "reveals that." Pattern consistency without a direct budget supports "is consistent with" or "suggests." Scaling or theoretical consistency supports "supports the interpretation that." Plausible mechanisms not directly diagnosed warrant "may reflect" or "could be related to."

The strongest JPO papers use strong language sparingly and match it to specific diagnostic evidence.
Overclaiming a mechanism without closure weakens the paper.

### Level of technicality

JPO is read by **physical oceanographers and other geophysical fluid dynamics specialists**.
Technical terminology is expected.
Equations can appear in the main text where they define the diagnostic framework.
Key principles:

- Define specialized terms on first use
- Equations should be accompanied by plain-language explanation of their physical meaning
- Acronyms should be minimized;
standard oceanographic abbreviations (EKE, PV, SST, MLD) are acceptable without definition only when well-established
- Mathematical formulations should serve the diagnostic argument, not display sophistication

### Mechanism language

Mechanism language must track the evidence.
Budget-based claims ("the momentum budget demonstrates that the pressure gradient force balances the Coriolis acceleration") are supported when budget terms are explicitly computed.
Energy-based claims ("baroclinic conversion KmKe is the dominant eddy energy source") are supported when conversion terms are diagnosed.
Correlative claims ("the spatial correspondence between EKE and baroclinic shear suggests baroclinic instability is active") are appropriate when the diagnostic chain is not closed.
Causal language ("drives", "controls") requires direct evidence from budget dominance, sensitivity experiments, or closed energy pathways.

### Preferred verb choice

JPO benefits from **physically specific verbs**: `accelerates, advects, mixes, shoals, deepens, steepens, flattens, weakens, intensifies, redistributes, converts, balances, constrains, extracts, injects, dissipates, enhances, suppresses, shifts, modulates, rectifies, sharpens, diffuses, stratifies, destratifies`.
These are stronger than vague verbs like `affects, influences, changes`.

### Broader significance framing

JPO significance is **internal to physical oceanography**.
Climate or ecosystem implications are secondary and should only be mentioned if directly supported by the analysis.
"This mechanism provides a dynamical explanation for the observed cross-front transport, with implications for the regional freshwater budget" is appropriate; "These results have implications for global climate projections" requires an explicit dynamical link.

## 6. Late-stage Compression and Submission Notes

### What to preserve during compression

- The diagnostic framework (key equations, budget terms, or scaling relationships)
- The evidence that directly supports the mechanism claim (dominant budget terms, energy conversion values, sensitivity tests)
- The mechanism interpretation in Discussion that completes the dynamical argument
- Figures that show the core diagnostics — budget closure, leading-order terms, or process-oriented analysis

### What to cut first if over length

- Extended background on the study region or well-known phenomena
- Secondary pattern descriptions that do not advance the mechanism
- Lengthy validation of standard methods (can move to supplementary material)
- Discussion of implications not directly connected to the diagnosed mechanism

### Hard constraints to verify

At the final pre-submission stage, check the journal's current author guidelines for word count limits, figure limits, and abstract length.
Apply these constraints only during the late polishing pass, not during drafting.
For AMS journals specifically: verify data availability and author contribution statements.

### Structure fit notes

- **Introduction frames a gap rather than a dynamical question:** If the Introduction says "few studies have examined" instead of "the controlling mechanism is unclear," the narrative may not be sharp enough for JPO.
- **Results organized as figure walkthrough rather than diagnostic progression:** Each results subsection should advance a step in the mechanism argument, not describe what a figure shows.
- **Discussion that restates results without mechanism closure:** If the Discussion section summarizes findings without addressing why the mechanism operates or what bounds it, the physical explanation is incomplete.
- **Methods that list data sources without connecting them to the diagnosis:** JPO Methods should show how each data type or model configuration supports the dynamical question.
- **Abstract that reports numbers without a physical finding:** If the abstract can be rewritten to describe data without mentioning a mechanism, the dynamical contribution may not be central enough for JPO.
