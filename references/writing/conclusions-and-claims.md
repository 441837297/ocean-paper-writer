# Conclusions and Claim Strength

## Purpose

This module is used during the 04 writing stage (Conclusion drafting)
and 05 review / 06 polish (claim boundary checking).
The Conclusion synthesizes supported findings, implications, limitations,
and future work. It must not introduce new evidence.

## No-new-evidence rule

The Conclusion must not introduce:
- new data not presented in Results
- new mechanisms not discussed in Discussion
- literature claims not appearing in Introduction or Discussion
- climate implications not supported by Results

## Finding / implication / speculation

| Type | What it can say | Required support | Risk |
|------|-----------------|------------------|------|
| Finding | A result presented in Results with figure/table evidence | Direct figure or data reference | Low |
| Implication | What the finding means for the broader system | Logic chain from finding + literature | Medium — must be bounded |
| Speculation | What might be true if the finding holds more broadly | Explicit hedging + stated uncertainty | High — label clearly |

Speculation is acceptable in the Conclusion only when explicitly flagged
("may", "could", "if confirmed by...", "remains to be tested").

## Claim strength ladder

| Wording | Strength | Requires |
|---------|----------|----------|
| observed | descriptive | direct observation or figure evidence |
| shows | moderate | direct evidence from analysis |
| suggests | cautious | pattern or partial support |
| is consistent with | cautious-mechanistic | supporting diagnostic or literature |
| indicates | moderate-strong | multiple converging evidence sources |
| supports | strong | clear evidence chain |
| demonstrates | strongest | robust evidence, tests, alternatives addressed |
| proves | avoid | almost never appropriate in empirical ocean science |

## Ocean/climate boundary rules

For each claim in the Conclusion, verify:

| Claim type | Boundary check |
|------------|---------------|
| regional result | Not presented as a global conclusion without cross-basin evidence. |
| short observational record | Not presented as a climate trend without detection study. |
| model-only result | Clearly labeled as model output, not observation. |
| correlation-only result | Not presented as causation without diagnostic support. |
| reanalysis result | Not presented as direct observation. |
| climate relevance | Not presented as climate-change evidence without attribution. |
| statistical significance | Not conflated with physical significance. |

## Conclusion structure

Recommended sequence:

1. **Central finding** — one sentence restating the primary result.
2. **Evidence basis** — which figures/methods/data support it.
3. **Bounded implication** — what the finding means, with explicit bounds.
4. **Limitation / uncertainty** — what cannot be concluded.
5. **Future work or next diagnostic** — specific, not generic.

## Example conclusion rewrites

**Risky:**
> This study demonstrates that Southern Ocean warming is accelerating
> and will have global consequences.

**Safer:**
> Satellite observations indicate that the Southern Ocean south of 50°S
> warmed at 0.12 ± 0.05°C per decade over 1993–2022 (Fig. 5b).
> This warming is concentrated in the upper 500 m and is most pronounced
> in the Pacific sector. If sustained, this trend may influence
> regional sea-ice extent, though circulation feedbacks
> remain unconstrained by the present analysis.

**Risky:**
> Mesoscale eddies play a key role in ocean heat transport.

**Safer (if supported by diagnostics):**
> Lagrangian eddy tracking reveals that long-lived anticyclonic eddies
> (lifetime > 30 days) account for 18 ± 5% of the total meridional
> heat transport across 40°S in the Indian Ocean sector (Fig. 7d).
> The contribution from shorter-lived eddies and cyclonic eddies
> is smaller (combined < 10%) and is not distinguishable
> from background variability at the 95% confidence level.
